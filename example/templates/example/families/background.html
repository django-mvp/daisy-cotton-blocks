Backgrounds

A background is not part of a layout, so it is not part of a block. Each one here is its own block that goes in another block's background slot, which means any background composes with any layout.

How the slot works

A hero is a stacking context, and whatever goes in the slot is rendered into a layer at -z-10 inside it: above the block's own surface colour, below every piece of copy. Nothing in the slot needs to know its own position or z-index.

Two rules that come with them

  • A background is decoration. The layer is hidden from assistive technology, so nothing in it is announced and <c-background.image> takes no alternative text. An image that carries meaning goes in the media slot of c-hero.split or c-hero.showcase, where it is a real <img> with an alt.
  • A dark background needs invert on the block. A background cannot reach up to recolour the copy above it, so the block carries the switch and each background below says whether it wants one.

Switch the theme in the bar above. Every one of these is built from the semantic palette, so the whole page should follow — and the copy should stay readable on all of them, which is the thing worth checking hardest.

<c-background.glow>

Two heavily blurred discs of the theme's own colours. Low opacity on purpose: the surface under the copy stays close to the page's own, so the text keeps the contrast it was designed for. Takes from, to and intensity. No invert.

<c-background.gradient>

A wash between two palette colours, in any of the eight directions. Takes from, via, to, direction and opacity. At opacity="full" it needs invert, and that combination is the one to check on a light theme as well as a dark one.

{# ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── grid #}

<c-background.grid>

A faint ruled grid. The rules are drawn in currentColor, so it inherits the block's own text colour and works on a light theme, a dark theme and behind invert without being told which. Takes size, intensity and flat. The quietest of the four, which is why it sits under dense copy better than a wash does.

{# ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── image #}

<c-background.image>

A picture, dimmed by the theme's own neutral. Always wants invert. Takes src, dim and position. Because the package cannot see the picture, dim is what makes the copy readable — treat "does this still read?" as the setting's whole purpose.

More than one layer

The slot takes markup, not just one block, so two backgrounds stack in the order they are written — later on top. Nothing special is needed for it to work, which is the useful consequence of the layer owning the positioning rather than the backgrounds doing it themselves.
