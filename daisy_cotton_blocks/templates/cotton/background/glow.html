No invert needed. #} {# @prop from:select['primary', 'secondary', 'accent', 'neutral', 'base-100', 'base-200', 'base-300'] | default:"primary" | description:"The disc at the top" #} {# @prop to:select['primary', 'secondary', 'accent', 'neutral', 'base-100', 'base-200', 'base-300'] | default:"secondary" | description:"The disc at the foot" #} {# @prop intensity:select['faint', 'soft', 'bold'] | default:"soft" | description:"How far the discs come through. Check bold against a dark theme." #} {# @prop class:text | description:"Extra classes on the layer" #} {% comment %} ============================================================ — two blurred discs of colour Goes in a block's `background` slot. Heavily blurred, low opacity, so the surface under the copy stays close to the page's own background and the text keeps the contrast it was designed for. That restraint is the whole point: colour on a saturated gradient is what fails on a theme you did not test. Every background block roots at `absolute inset-0` rather than filling its parent in normal flow, which is what lets two of them be written into the same slot and land on top of each other. Two block-level layers in flow would stack one *below* the other and the second would never be seen. So a background belongs in a `background` slot and nowhere else — the slot's wrapper is what it positions against. ============================================================ {% endcomment %}
