{# @description A wash between two palette colours. Needs invert at full opacity. #} {# @prop from:select['primary', 'secondary', 'accent', 'neutral', 'base-100', 'base-200', 'base-300'] | default:"primary" | description:"The first stop" #} {# @prop via:select['primary', 'secondary', 'accent', 'neutral', 'base-100', 'base-200', 'base-300'] | description:"An optional middle stop" #} {# @prop to:select['primary', 'secondary', 'accent', 'neutral', 'base-100', 'base-200', 'base-300'] | default:"secondary" | description:"The last stop" #} {# @prop direction:select['t', 'tr', 'r', 'br', 'b', 'bl', 'l', 'tl'] | default:"br" | description:"Which way the wash runs" #} {# @prop opacity:select['faint', 'soft', 'full'] | default:"soft" | description:"How much of the block's own surface shows through. full needs invert." #} {# @prop class:text | description:"Extra classes on the layer" #} {% comment %} ============================================================ — a wash between two theme colours Goes in a block's `background` slot. Read the contrast warning before reaching for a saturated pair. Copy sitting on a full-strength `primary → secondary` gradient fails WCAG's 4.5:1 on several of daisyUI's own themes, because `primary` is a light colour in some of them. Two ways out, both supported here: - Keep `opacity` at its default, so the wash sits over the block's own surface and the copy keeps the contrast measured for that surface. - Or go full strength and set `invert` on the block, which puts the copy in `neutral-content`. Check it against a light theme and a dark one. Roots at `absolute inset-0`, like every background block, so that two written into the same slot land on top of each other rather than one below the other. See for the reasoning. A background belongs in a `background` slot and nowhere else. ============================================================ {% endcomment %}
